# Description
---
A cataract located at the **Posterior Pole of the Lens**, just in front of the posterior capsule. It forms from abnormal migration or clumping of lens epithelial cells, creating a **Dense, Granular Opacity** that scatters light strongly. PSCs disproportionately affect **Near Vision, Glare, and Bright‑Light Tasks**, even when small.

# TLDR
---
- Central Vision
- Posterior Pole, Lens Capsule 
- Bubble-like Appearance
- Shearing of Lens Fibers
- Astigmatic Shift
- Increased Refractive Index
# Linked Symptoms
---
- [[Glare|Glare Sensitivity]]
	- Due to light scatter with oncoming headlights or sunlight
- **Reduced near vision** out of proportion to distance vision
- **Difficulty reading in bright environments**
- **Hazy or washed‑out vision**
- **Reduced contrast**
- **Vision that worsens quickly over months** (faster progression than nuclear cataracts)
# Risk Factors
---
- **Chronic steroid use** (topical, inhaled, oral, systemic)
- **Radiation exposure**
- **Diabetes**
- **Uveitis / chronic intraocular inflammation**
- **High myopia**
- **Trauma**
- **Post‑surgical changes** (e.g., post‑vitrectomy)
# Pathophysiology
---
The pathophysiology of PSC is mainly due to the first mechanism:
1. Change in growth factors (ie. FGF) causing undifferentiated cell migration to post pole (leads to PSC formation)
	- Aberrant lens epithelial cell proliferation at germinative zone and migration to the posterior pole
	- Cells collate with adjacent lens fibres & form balloon cells
	- Fail to differentiate & lose organelles, produce ECM and have abnormal Na+/K+ ATPase active transport functioning
	- Lens fibre swelling (osmotic pressure), formation of intercellular and interfibrous vacuoles and production of extracellular granular and fibrillary material 
	- Risk factors = diabetes, corticosteroid use, radiation, uveitis, aging
