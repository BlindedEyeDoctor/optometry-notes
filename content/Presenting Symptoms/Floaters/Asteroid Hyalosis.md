# Description
Asteroid hyalosis is usually **benign** and **non‑progressive**.
# Diagnostic Testing 
- Presentation/History Taking
	- PX would report mild blur or many moving shadows
- Recommended Tests
	- Visual Acuity
		- Assess vision quality
	- Biomicroscopy |Slit lamp external exam
		- Looking for Schafer's sign 
		- Assessing vitreous movement, 
		- anterior segment cells, flare, KPs
		- posterior ca spill
		- RDs can cause a low grade iridocyclitis.
	- Fundoscopy|Dilated Fundus Exam
		- Retinal traction, tears, degeneration 
		- Vitreous Occlusion
	- Pupil Testing
		- Look for Neurological Damage
	- Optical Coherence Tomography | OCT
		- Look for Retinal Detachment
	- Visual Fields
		- Look for loss of visual fields, indicative of Retinal Detachment
	- Tonometry | IOP Assessment 
		- Look for high and low IOP
# Primary Associated Disease
- **Asteroid Hyalosis** Calcium‑lipid deposits suspended in vitreous; shimmering floaters.
# Differential Diagnosis
Not causative, but statistically more common in:
- **Diabetes Mellitus**
- **Hypertension**
- **Hyperlipidemia**
# Rare Associated Ocular Conditions
- **Vitreous Degeneration / Syneresis** If asteroid is dense enough to cause visual disturbance.
- **Reduced fundus view** (for clinician) Not a patient symptom, but important for exam quality.
# Associated Symptoms
- Shimmering or sparkling floaters
- Usually **no blur**
- Usually **no flashes**
- Often **stable for years**
