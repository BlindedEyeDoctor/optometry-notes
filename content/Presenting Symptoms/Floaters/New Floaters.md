# Description

Your brain is very good at ignoring floaters, so you would know if a new one pops into view.
# Diagnostic Testing 
- Presentation/History Taking
	- PX reports new floaters 
- Recommended Tests
	- Visual Acuity
		- Assess vision quality
	- Biomicroscopy |Slit lamp external exam
		- Looking for Schafer's sign 
		- Assessing vitreous movement, 
		- anterior segment cells, flare, KPs
		- posterior ca spill
		- RDs can cause a low grade iridocyclitis.
	- Fundoscopy|Dilated Fundus Exam
		- Retinal traction, tears, degeneration 
		- Vitreous Occlusion
	- Pupil Testing
		- Look for Neurological Damage
	- Optical Coherence Tomography | OCT
		- Look for Retinal Detachment
	- Visual Fields
		- Look for loss of visual fields, indicative of Retinal Detachment
	- Tonometry | IOP Assessment 
		- Look for high and low IOP
# High‑Yield Associated Diseases*
- **Posterior Vitreous Detachment (PVD)** Most common cause of sudden increased floaters.
- **Retinal Tear** Increased floaters + flashes = high suspicion.
- **Retinal Detachment** Increased floaters + flashes + curtain.
- **Vitreous Hemorrhage** Sudden cloud of floaters, red haze.
- **Uveitis (Vitritis)** White inflammatory floaters + photophobia + pain.
- **Diabetic Retinopathy** Floaters from microbleeds or vitreous hemorrhage.
- **Vitreous Syneresis** Age‑related liquefaction → chronic increased floaters.
# Associated Symptoms
- Flashes (traction)
- Blurred vision
- Red haze (hemorrhage)
- Photophobia (uveitis)
- Field defects (tear/detachment)
- Sudden onset (PVD, tear, hemorrhage)
- Gradual onset (syneresis)
