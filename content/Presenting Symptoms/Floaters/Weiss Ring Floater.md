# Description
A Weiss ring = **posterior vitreous detachment (PVD)** until proven otherwise.
# Diagnostic Testing 
- Presentation/History Taking
	- PX reports vision loss for hours before it returns abruptly
- Recommended Tests
	- Visual Acuity
		- Assess vision quality
	- Biomicroscopy |Slit lamp external exam
		- Looking for Schafer's sign 
		- Assessing vitreous movement, 
		- anterior segment cells, flare, KPs
		- posterior ca spill
		- RDs can cause a low grade iridocyclitis.
	- Fundoscopy|Dilated Fundus Exam
		- Retinal traction, tears, degeneration 
		- Vitreous Occlusion
	- Pupil Testing
		- Look for Neurological Damage
	- Optical Coherence Tomography | OCT
		- Look for Retinal Detachment
	- Visual Fields
		- Look for loss of visual fields, indicative of Retinal Detachment
	- Tonometry | IOP Assessment 
		- Look for high and low IOP
# Primary Associated Disease
- **Posterior Vitreous Detachment (PVD)** Sudden onset floater, ring‑shaped, moves with eye movement.
# Differential Diagnosis
- **Retinal Tear** If accompanied by **flashes**, **new floaters**, or **Shafer’s sign** (tobacco dust).
- **Retinal Detachment** If patient reports **curtain**, **field loss**, or **progressive flashes**.
- **Vitreous Hemorrhage** If traction from PVD causes a retinal vessel to break.
# Associated Symptoms
- Flashes (photopsia)
- Sudden onset floater
- Cobweb shadow
- Mild blur
- Possible peripheral field defect (if tear/detachment)
