# Description
---
Clear, thin, watery discharge caused by **Excess Tear Production** or **Tear Film Instability**. Common in **Viral Infections, Allergy, Irritation, Dry Eye**, or early inflammatory responses. Unlike mucous or purulent discharge, watery discharge does **Not** contain significant cellular debris.
# Associated Symptoms
---
- Tearing / watering
- Mild burning or irritation
- Grittiness
- Itching (allergy)
- Photophobia (viral)
- Foreign body sensation
- Eyelid puffiness
- Occasional redness
# Presentation
---
- PX reports **constant watering**
- PX reports **tears running down the cheek**
- PX may note **mild crusting** upon waking (from dried tears, not mucus)
- Eyes appear **glossy** or overly moist
- Redness may be present depending on cause
# Recommended Testing 
---
- Biomicroscopy
    - Assess conjunctiva, cornea, eyelid margin
    - Look for follicles, papillae, punctate staining
    - Check tear meniscus height
- Fluorescein staining
    - Evaluate tear breakup time (TBUT)
    - Rule out epithelial defects
- Eyelid eversion
    - Check for papillae (allergy) or follicles (viral)
- Lacrimal drainage assessment (if excessive tearing)
    - Puncta patency, regurgitation test
# Differential Diagnosis
---
- **Viral Conjunctivitis**
    - Classic **watery** discharge + follicles + photophobia.
- **Allergic Conjunctivitis**
    - Watery discharge with itching and papillae.
- **Dry Eye Disease**
    - Reflex tearing from tear film instability; burning/grittiness.
- **Irritative Conjunctivitis**
    - Wind, smoke, chemical exposure → watery discharge.
- **Nasolacrimal Duct Obstruction**
    - Constant tearing; tears spill over despite normal ocular surface.
- **Early Bacterial Conjunctivitis**
    - Can begin watery before becoming mucopurulent.
